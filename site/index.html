A big monthly number in is not the same as a cushion. Twin Runway shows both, on the account's own figures.

How it works

The judges' number first, then the real world, then the plan

  1. The judges' number first

    Runway is the judges' formula, exactly as the track brief defines it: (buffer − one-off) ÷ (spending after the shock − money in after the shock). The twist cards apply the judges' shocks: laid off on EI at 55%, a furnace repair of one month's spending, or a job loss with household bills up 15%.

  2. Labelled reality checks beside it

    Switchable checks sit next to the official number, each labelled as our assumption, so the judges' number never changes: the real 2026 EI cap ($729 a week), keeping a pension on job loss, typical half-year spending, bills paid by standing order, and a month-by-month start in January.

  3. A goal plan and a backup plan

    The goal plan sizes a job-loss-proof fund that passes the shock on the judges' formula, and fills it with an automatic monthly transfer of the normal surplus. The backup plan is a ladder of steps, cheapest first, that stops at the first one reaching 6 months. Across the portfolio, 1,192 of the 1,555 failing accounts that still have a buffer get back to 6 months by cutting a quarter of spending or less.

The early-warning light

A watch flag beside the runway, not a credit score. It gives one point for each of 4 rules over the account's last 6 months, and from 2 points the apps show it as a warning:

  • lowest balance under half a month of spending
  • almost all spending is cash
  • irregular money in
  • balance sliding more than 10% of a month's spending per month

The 2025 evidence

The judges' test misses people. The light catches more of them.

Every score is taken as it stood at the 2024-12 snapshot, on the 3,847 accounts rated then and still active in 2025. Of those, 75 first went overdrawn in 2025, and 28 of those 75 had passed the judges' 6-month test at the snapshot.

A machine-learning check, worded honestly

Gradient boosting on 13 features, trained on 2024's outcomes, catches 47 on the same 10% watch-list: about as many overdrafts as the light, but not all the same ones. Both lists catch 38 of them, and each catches 9 the other misses. This data can't tell the two rankings apart, so both apps keep the 4 rules anyone can read.

How it's built

One formula, three languages, the same judges' numbers

The judges' numbers match on all 4,500 accounts in Python, TypeScript and SQL (DuckDB).

Python engine

One deterministic engine computes every number: the judges' runway, the shocks, the reality checks, the plans, the light and the backtest. The test suite recomputes every figure the docs and this page quote.

React and TypeScript app

The engine, ported to TypeScript, runs in the browser offline: no keys, no network. Parity tests check it against the Python engine's outputs.

SQL in DuckDB and Snowflake

The judges' numbers as plain SQL, run offline in DuckDB or in a Snowflake warehouse.

Optional Gemini wording

In the Streamlit app, Gemini can reword the story over the engine's numbers. It never computes a number: if its text has a figure the engine didn't give it, the app shows its built-in template instead.
